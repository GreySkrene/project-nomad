# Midland ER10VP offline quick reference

Verified 2026-11-07 against Midland's ER10 owner manual dated 2025-10-07 and
the NOAA Weather Radio national station brochure dated July 2025. Transmitter
status and reception are time-sensitive; confirm live when communications are
available.

## Fast setup

1. Install three AA alkaline batteries.
2. Hold the power button for three seconds.
3. Press BAND until `WX` appears.
4. Fully extend and rotate the antenna. Use the tune buttons, or hold one to
   scan, until you find the clearest local broadcast.
5. Press the alert button. A flashing `WX` icon means alerts are armed. The
   manual says alerts can remain enabled while the radio is powered off.
6. When an alert arrives, the siren and flashlight activate. Press any button
   to cancel the alert.

The ER10 manual provides no SAME/FIPS county-programming feature. It is a
channel-based tone-alert receiver, so it may alert for any warning carried by
the selected transmitter—not only your county.

## Regional starting points

These are starting points, not reception guarantees. Mountains can sharply
reduce or redirect coverage. Scan all seven channels at the actual location and
choose the clearest broadcast that names the county or forecast area you need.

| Area | First station(s) to try | ER10 channel |
|---|---|---:|
| Salt Lake Valley / West Jordan / Riverton | Salt Lake City, 162.550 | 7 |
| Davis and Weber counties | Salt Lake City, 162.550; Logan, 162.400 | 7, then 1 |
| Marysvale / Piute County | Manti or Escalante, 162.425; Castle Dale, 162.500 | 2, then 5 |
| Rexburg / Madison County | Pocatello, 162.550; Driggs, 162.450; Sedgwick Peak, 162.425 | 7, 3, then 2 |

For travel, rescan after crossing mountain passes or moving roughly 40–50
miles. NOAA says practical range varies with terrain, antenna placement,
receiver quality, and transmitter power.

## Functional check

- NOAA requires routine alert tests on Wednesdays, generally between 10:00 AM
  and 1:00 PM local time, unless threatening weather causes postponement.
- At least monthly, confirm clear audio on the selected channel, arm alert mode,
  check the battery indicator, test both flashlight levels and SOS mode, and
  inspect the battery compartment for leakage.
- Keep one fresh set of three AA batteries with the radio and another sealed
  spare set in the emergency kit. Remove batteries for long-term unattended
  storage if leakage would go unnoticed.
- Keep another warning method available. A single transmitter may be out of
  service or blocked by terrain.
