# FRS family radio quick reference

This guide applies to certified 22-channel Family Radio Service handhelds in
the United States. It does not replace a model-specific manual.

## Before an emergency

- Choose one primary channel and one backup channel. Record both on every
  family's paper communication plan.
- Choose a check-in schedule, such as the top of every hour, to avoid leaving
  radios powered continuously.
- Practice from the locations that matter. Buildings, hills, valleys, and
  foliage reduce range dramatically; package “maximum range” claims assume
  unusually clear line of sight.
- Label each radio and battery/charger. Store spare charged packs or the exact
  manufacturer-approved replacement batteries.

## Operating procedure

1. Listen before transmitting.
2. Hold the radio upright, a few inches from your mouth. Press push-to-talk,
   pause briefly, then speak slowly and clearly.
3. Identify the person you are calling, then yourself: “Base, this is Drew.”
4. Keep messages short. Say location, condition, need, and next action.
5. Repeat critical numbers and have the receiver read them back.
6. End with “out” and return to the planned listening schedule.

FRS has no nationally reserved emergency channel. If immediate help is needed,
call 911 whenever service exists; otherwise use the agreed family channel and
clearly say “emergency.” Other users may be able to hear. “Privacy codes” only
filter which transmissions open your speaker; they do not encrypt speech.

## Channels and limits

- FRS has 22 shared channels; interference is normal.
- Channels 8–14 are limited to 0.5 W effective radiated power.
- Channels 1–7 and 15–22 allow certified FRS radios up to 2 W.
- FRS radios use their integral, non-removable antenna. Do not modify it.
- FRS normally requires no individual FCC license when using a certified FRS
  radio as designed. A radio transmitting under GMRS rules can require a GMRS
  license even when channel numbers overlap.

## Weather reception

NOAA Weather Radio uses seven receive-only frequencies: 162.400, 162.425,
162.450, 162.475, 162.500, 162.525, and 162.550 MHz. A radio's displayed WX
channel number may map to these frequencies differently by manufacturer.
Transmitter outages and warnings are time-sensitive and require live
verification when communications are available.
