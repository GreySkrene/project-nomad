# Emergency equipment inspection checklist

Put the actual brand, model, serial number, battery type, and purchase date on
each item's label. Photograph rating labels and store the pictures with its
manual.

## Monthly during high-risk seasons

- Power station and power banks: inspect for swelling, cracks, heat damage,
  odor, damaged ports, and recalled model numbers. Stop using damaged or
  recalled lithium products.
- Flashlights and radios: test every mode briefly; check contacts for corrosion
  and replace leaking cells using the exact battery type and orientation.
- Butane canisters: inspect for rust, dents, damaged rims/valves, or odor. Do not
  test for leaks with a flame. Isolate a suspect canister outdoors away from
  people and ignition, then follow local hazardous-waste guidance.
- Stove: inspect seals and the canister-lock mechanism. Do not connect fuel
  until the exact stove manual has been saved and reviewed.
- Water filters: check packaging, freeze exposure, expiration dates where
  applicable, and manufacturer-specified backflush/storage requirements.
- First-aid kit: replace opened, wet, damaged, recalled, or expired supplies.

## Every three months

- Recharge stored power banks according to their manuals and record the date.
- Check the Jackery Explorer 600 v2 and return it to its manufacturer-recommended
  storage range; Jackery recommends checking/recharging about every three
  months.
- Rotate alkaline batteries so the oldest serviceable stock is used first.
- Recheck family radio channels, paper contact plans, and NOAA reception at the
  locations where the radios will be used.

## Every 6–12 months

- Run a short power-outage drill with internet and utility power disconnected.
- Perform the Jackery manual's recommended full charge/discharge maintenance
  cycle, without exceeding load or temperature limits.
- Re-read product-recall lists while internet is available.
- Repack emergency bags, update clothing sizes and medications, and weigh each
  bag after repacking.
- Replace printed quick-reference cards when source guidance or equipment
  models change.

## Storage rules

- Keep lithium batteries and power stations cool, dry, protected from crushing,
  and away from fire or direct sun. Use compatible chargers and cables.
- Prevent loose battery terminals from contacting metal objects. Never mix old
  and new cells, chemistries, capacities, or brands in one device unless its
  manufacturer explicitly permits it.
- Store fuel outside living areas in a cool, ventilated location allowed by the
  product label and local fire code, away from ignition and children.
- Never operate a butane/propane stove, charcoal device, or generator indoors,
  in a garage, tent, or near building openings. Carbon monoxide is odorless and
  can be fatal.

Sources: CPSC battery and portable-fuel safety guidance; FEMA power-outage
guidance; Jackery JE-600A manufacturer documentation; CDC water-treatment
guidance.
