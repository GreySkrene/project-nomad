# CPR and severe choking — 2025 AHA emergency quick reference

This is a memory aid, not a substitute for hands-on CPR/first-aid training.
Call 911 and follow the dispatcher whenever service is available. Treat a
person who is only gasping as not breathing normally.

## Severe choking: adult or child older than 1 year

Signs include inability to speak, cry, or cough effectively; little or no air
movement; color change; or altered responsiveness.

1. Call 911 or direct someone else to call and retrieve an AED.
2. Give **5 back blows**, followed by **5 abdominal thrusts**.
3. Repeat alternating sets of five until the object comes out or the person
   becomes unresponsive.
4. For late pregnancy, or if you cannot encircle the abdomen, use **5 chest
   thrusts** instead of abdominal thrusts.
5. If the person becomes unresponsive, lower them safely and begin CPR. Before
   breaths, remove an object only if you can see it. Do not perform blind finger
   sweeps.

If the person can cough or speak, encourage coughing and watch continuously;
do not begin thrusts unless the obstruction becomes severe.

## Severe choking: infant younger than 1 year

1. Call 911 or direct someone else to call.
2. Support the infant face-down with the head lower than the chest. Give **5
   back blows** between the shoulder blades.
3. Turn the infant face-up while continuing to support the head lower than the
   chest. Give **5 chest thrusts using the heel of one hand** on the lower half
   of the sternum.
4. Repeat alternating sets of five until the object comes out or the infant
   becomes unresponsive. Do not use abdominal thrusts on an infant.
5. If unresponsive, begin infant CPR. Remove an object only when visible; never
   perform a blind finger sweep.

The 2025 guideline no longer recommends the old two-finger technique for
infant CPR compressions. Use the heel of one hand or the two-thumb encircling
hands technique if you are trained to do so.

## Adult or teen sudden collapse: hands-only CPR

1. Confirm the scene is safe. Tap and shout. If the person is unresponsive and
   not breathing normally or is only gasping, call 911 and get an AED.
2. Put the person on their back on a firm, flat surface.
3. Place two hands in the center of the chest. Lock your elbows with shoulders
   over your hands.
4. Push hard and fast at **100–120 compressions per minute**, at least **2
   inches (5 cm)** deep while avoiding more than 2.4 inches (6 cm). Let the
   chest fully recoil after every compression.
5. Use the AED as soon as it arrives and follow its prompts. Continue until the
   person moves, breathes normally, or trained help takes over.

For infants, children, drowning, overdose, or breathing-caused cardiac arrest,
compressions with breaths are important. Use dispatcher instructions and your
most recent formal training.
